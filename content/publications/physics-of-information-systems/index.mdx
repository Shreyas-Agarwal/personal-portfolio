# Overview

Every information system — a database, a sensor network, a ledger, a distributed cache — stands
between two things that are never the same: the reality it is trying to describe, and the signs it
stores in its place. This publication starts from that gap and works outward, one monograph at a
time, toward the architectural consequences of never being able to close it.

<Observation n={1}>
  A system never stores reality. It stores signs — symbols, tokens, encodings — that a community of
  use has agreed to treat as standing for reality. Every downstream architectural decision inherits
  the consequences of that substitution.
</Observation>

## The Reality Monograph

The first monograph, **Reality**, establishes the ground the rest of the series is built on: that a
representation is categorically distinct from what it represents, that every representation is a
lossy projection of a more complex territory, and that the complexity left out by that projection
does not vanish — it is redistributed, not eliminated.

- **01. Reality Exists Before Information** — On the categorical gap between a sign and its referent, and why no amount of speed or fidelity closes it.
- **02. The Territory Exists Before the Map** — Why every model is downstream of reality, and why representation is always lossy by design, not by accident.
- **03. Complexity Never Disappears** — On the conservation law that closes the monograph: complexity redistributed across abstraction layers, never eliminated.
