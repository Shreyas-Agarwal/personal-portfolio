# Why Analytics Platforms Require a Canonical Business Model

Many organizations begin by believing their reporting environment is simply a reflection of their operational systems. The CRM defines customers, the project management platform defines projects, the scheduling tool defines schedules, and dashboards simply visualize the data.

Initially, this works. Then the questions start changing.

<Observation n={1}>
  Operational systems are designed to operate workflows. Analytics platforms are designed to understand businesses. Those are not the same objective.
</Observation>

---

## The Shift From Visibility to Understanding

Most analytics initiatives begin with simple visibility: *How many projects are active? How many open issues exist?*

```text
Operational System → Dashboard
```

As organizations mature, leadership stops asking *what is happening* and begins asking *why is it happening*:

- Which project characteristics consistently correlate with schedule delays?
- Which issue categories create the greatest downstream financial impact?
- Which leading indicators predict future delivery failures?

These questions require relationships, historical context, cross-system analysis, derived metrics, and business definitions that operational schemas were never designed to express.

---

## The Visualization Layer Starts Absorbing Business Logic

When operational schemas cannot answer business questions directly, logic quietly migrates into the BI layer:

- A Power Query transformation is added.
- A calculated column appears.
- A DAX measure is introduced.
- Custom aggregations multiply.

<Callout type="warning">
  When business logic is scattered across Power Query transformations, DAX measures, and report-level calculations, the BI tool stops visualizing data and starts silently transforming data.
</Callout>

### The Silicon Ceiling of BI Tools

Power BI, Tableau, and Looker are visualization tools. They are not intended to function as:

1. Transformation engines
2. Data integration platforms
3. Business rules repositories
4. Enterprise semantic architectures

When forced into all four roles, refresh cycles expand, performance deteriorates, and the organization suffers an analytical bottleneck.

---

## The Canonical Business Model

Mature analytics platforms resolve this by making an architectural transition. They stop asking *what does the vendor call this?* and start asking *what does the business call this?*

<Decision n={1} status="accepted">
  Establish a Canonical Business Model. Define vendor-agnostic entities (Project, Schedule, Contract, Asset, Customer) in a centralized transformation layer, decoupling analytical consumers from upstream SaaS schemas.
</Decision>

```text
Operational Systems
        ↓
Canonical Business Model
        ↓
Transformation Layer (dbt / SQL)
        ↓
Semantic Layer
        ↓
Visualization / BI
```

<Tradeoff
  gain="Buffers analytical consumers from vendor API updates, field deprecations, and schema drift."
  cost="Requires maintaining an explicit transformation and semantic layer outside the BI tool."
/>
